This project was my senior thesis capstone project for my degree at Rose-Hulman. I worked on this project with a team of 3 other students, David Gottlieb, Dylan Lutrell, and Helen Wang, under the supervision of Dr. Yoder. In this project we ambitiously tried to do three difficult things simultaniously, hoping that they would synergize. These three dimensions to the research were:

  • Improving upon developmental neural architectures
  • Using uncertainty estimation to guide learning
  • Using adaptive hyperparameter optimization to adjust learning parameters on the fly

While we all influenced all parts of the system, I was mainly responisble for the adaptive hyperparameter optimization feature. This subsystem was initially inspired by my previous research with Dr. Yoder on developmental learning. Despite this inspiration the idea pivoted from an evolutionary system to a RL agent which recieved rewards based on the testing performance of the main system. While a few experiments showed some promise, the overall system was not really able to compete with previous methods and no publication came of this research. Despite the lack of success, I learned a lot from this experience about scope management, academic research, hyperparameter optimization and reinforcement learning.

In the 2022-23 school year, as part of my undergraduate studies at Rose Hulman, I took part in undergraduate research projects with Dr. Jason Yoder and my peer Jacob Ashworth. In this research we used the NK Fitness Landscape model to simulate and increase our understanding of the interaction between Evolution and Development in population optimization. This research is expanded on the research published in Artificial Life in 2022, by Yoder et.al. I worked to produce significant performance improvements, dynamic landscapes, a wider variety of summary statistics, and a more robust architecture. We used tools to study the ways in which learning strategies develop in a variety of scenarios, and to learn about how various aspects of the environement impact the evolution of development. I then worked with Dr. Yoder and Jacob Ashworth to document and publish our work in Artificial Life 2023. This work also partially served as inspiration for my senior thesis research on dynamic hyperparameter optimization in machine learning.

In this project I worked with a relatively large team (for a school project) of about 10-20 students with direct oversight from Dr. Shibberu and Dr. Boutell to produce an AI chatbot which could assist freshmen with certain advisory tasks. The goal of this project was to alliveate some of the existing pressure on freshman advisors by redirecting some more easily answerable questions to a chatbot. To ensure the utility of this system, we conducted some software requirements engineering to learn from freshmen and advisors what would be neccesary from such a system. This system used RAG and system prompting to retrieve information relevant to student queries to ensure more reliable answers to student questions. Unfortunately despite our teams best efforts we were unable to prevent the system from hallucintating or providing incorrect feedback. This may is likely due to intentionally using smaller models for budgetary reasons and attempting to compensate using RAG and prompting. While the system never saw widespread use or adoption due to concerns over reliablity of responses, working on this project and with this team taught me a lot about working with LLM integrated systems.

As a final project for a deep learning course at Rose Hulman, I worked with a team to predict missing salary information from linkedin job postings from a Kaggle Dataset. Due to the formatings of the postings we had to do a lot of data preprocessing to make the dataset usable, such as filtering out inaccurate data, vectorizing text features, one hot and multi-hot encodings and PCA. In the end using a deep learning model with L2 regularization, masking, custom loss functions, and batch normalization we were able to get a reasonably accurate prediction of salaries given incomplete data.

Queue is a web application dedicated to helping users prioritize their tasks using computer science principles and the priority queue data structure. Users are able to input thier obligations into the website, and select a function for the priority of the project in the queue. These functions operate on the variables of the item in order to prioritize the queue on each refresh. This allows items with sooner due dates to pass those of higher priority as the due dates arrive. Power users can also choose to define their own functions in order to better fit their own needs.
